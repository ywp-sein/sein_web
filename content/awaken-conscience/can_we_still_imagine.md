There is something fascinating about learning in the age of AI. Technology can now show us things that once required enormous effort to understand.

Think about mathematics.

When we learn an equation, we can draw it on an XY plane and slowly understand what that equation actually represents. Today, a computer can visualize it for us instantly. Change a variable, and the graph changes in front of our eyes. Move from two dimensions to three, and something that was difficult to picture suddenly becomes visible.

This is clearly progress.

But I think there is another question worth asking:

- **After the computer shows it to us, can we close the screen and still see it in our minds?**
- Can we develop an intuition for how the equation behaves?
- Can we imagine what happens when one variable changes without immediately asking a machine to show us?
- Can we take what technology has helped us see and turn it into something we truly understand?

I think we can. And perhaps this is one of the most important ways we should think about AI and learning.

AI should not replace our ability to think and imagine. It should help us develop that ability further.

## From Visualization to Imagination
This idea goes far beyond mathematics.

AI is becoming extraordinarily good at making things visible. We can describe an idea and generate an image. We can describe a system and ask for a simulation. We can give AI a hypothesis and ask it to analyze whether it makes sense.

Things that once existed only in our imagination can increasingly be turned into something we can see, test, and discuss.

But that creates an interesting paradox.

**When everything becomes easier to visualize, will we become better at imagining—or will we gradually imagine less?**

There is a difference between visualization and imagination.

Visualization shows us something that can already be represented.

Imagination asks:

**What could exist that nobody has represented yet?**

That is where human curiosity becomes incredibly important.

## Some Ideas Should Sound Crazy
Not every valuable idea begins by making sense.

Sometimes curiosity takes us somewhere strange.

- What if this were possible?
- What if we ignored this assumption?
- What if something everyone considers impossible is only impossible because nobody has found another way yet?

Today, we have an extraordinary new partner for exploring those questions.

We can take a crazy idea and give it to AI.

We can say:

- Help me examine this.
- Challenge my assumptions.
- Tell me what would have to be true for this to work.
- Show me where it fails.
- Show me what I haven't considered.

That is a very different relationship with AI from simply asking it for the answer.

AI becomes a tool for extending curiosity rather than replacing it.

And I believe this distinction will matter enormously.

## Don't Let Probability Become the Boundary of Possibility
AI is built from what already exists: data, patterns, relationships, examples, and accumulated human knowledge.

That makes it incredibly powerful.

But human imagination has an unusual ability.

- **We can imagine things that do not yet make sense.**
- We can question the assumptions behind the problem itself.
- We can dream about something before we know how to build it.

Of course, most crazy ideas will remain crazy ideas. Some will be impossible. Some will fail immediately when confronted with physics, economics, or reality.

That's fine.

The point of imagination is not that every dream must become reality.

The point is that we should not stop dreaming simply because a system trained on what already exists considers something unlikely.

AI can help us determine whether an idea is possible.

But perhaps we should first give ourselves permission to imagine it.

## The People Who Will Make a Difference
This is why I don't believe AI itself will limit human imagination. 

But **the way we use AI will**.

If we surrender every question to AI, we may become excellent consumers of answers.

But if we use AI to strengthen our understanding, challenge our thinking, test our ideas, and explore our curiosity, something very different can happen.

The distance between **imagining something** and **testing it** is becoming dramatically shorter.

Think about someone who understands the fundamentals of their field deeply, has trained themselves to think independently, remains intensely curious, and is still willing to dream about things that sound unreasonable.

Now give that person AI.

That combination can make a real difference.

AI can show us what we already know. It can calculate what is probable. It can help us test what is possible.

**But we still have to imagine what does not exist yet.**

So as AI becomes more capable, the question for us is not only how well we can use it.

It is whether we can preserve—and strengthen—the curiosity, imagination, and courage that make us human.

When knowledge is everywhere, when answers are instant, and when AI can visualize almost anything we ask of it:

**Do we still dare to dream big?**
